Unit I — Foundations of Information Security ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Establish the essential concepts, terminology, and principles needed before studying technical security controls.

Module 1. Introduction to Information Assurance and Security
Module description: Introduces information assurance, information security, cybersecurity, and the principles used to protect information assets.
Lesson / subtopic	Description
1.1 Information Security Fundamentals	Defines information security, cybersecurity, information assets, and the basic concepts used to protect systems and information.
1.2 Information Assurance Principles	Explains how security controls, validation, risk management, and recovery help maintain trustworthy information.
1.3 Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability	Introduces the CIA triad and its role in preventing disclosure, unauthorized modification, and service disruption.
1.4 Authenticity	Explains how to verify that information, users, devices, and communications are genuine.
1.5 Accountability	Describes how actions are recorded and attributed to responsible users or systems.
1.6 Non-repudiation	Explains how evidence can establish that an action or transaction occurred and help prevent its denial.
1.7 Privacy	Introduces the protection of personal information and responsible data handling.
1.8 Security Objectives and Trade-offs	Explores how organizations balance security, usability, cost, and performance.

Module 2. Security Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Attacks
Module description: Explains how security incidents occur, what weaknesses attackers exploit, and how common attack methods affect information systems.
Lesson / subtopic	Description
2.1 Threats, Vulnerabilities, and Risk	Differentiates potential sources of harm, system weaknesses, and the risks resulting from their interaction.
2.2 Malware	Introduces viruses, worms, Trojans, spyware, ransomware, and other malicious software.
2.3 Phishing and Social Engineering	Explains how attackers manipulate people into disclosing information or performing unsafe actions.
2.4 Password and Credential Attacks	Covers brute-force attacks, credential stuffing, password spraying, and credential theft.
2.5 Denial-of-Service Attacks	Explains how DoS and DDoS attacks disrupt the availability of services.
2.6 Man-in-the-Middle Attacks	Describes how attackers intercept or manipulate communications between parties.
2.7 Spoofing and Impersonation	Explains the falsification of identities, email addresses, websites, and communications.
2.8 Insider Threats	Examines security risks caused by malicious, negligent, or compromised authorized users.
2.9 Zero-Day Vulnerabilities	Introduces flaws exploited before an effective patch or fix is available.
2.10 Advanced Persistent Threats	Examines targeted, sustained intrusions intended to maintain access or achieve long-term objectives.
2.11 Web Application Attacks	Introduces SQL injection, XSS, CSRF, and other common application-level attack techniques.
2.12 Supply-Chain Attacks	Explains how compromised vendors, dependencies, or software delivery processes can affect downstream organizations.
2.13 Physical Security Threats	Covers theft, unauthorized entry, tampering, and environmental events that threaten information assets.

Module 3. Information Security Risk Management
Module description: Teaches learners to identify, analyze, prioritize, treat, and monitor risks to organizational information systems.
Lesson / subtopic	Description
3.1 Risk Identification	Identifies assets, threats, vulnerabilities, and conditions that could affect organizational objectives.
3.2 Risk Analysis	Estimates the likelihood and consequences of security incidents.
3.3 Qualitative Risk Assessment	Uses descriptive ratings, such as low, medium, and high, to evaluate risks.
3.4 Quantitative Risk Assessment	Uses numerical estimates to express risk exposure and potential financial impact.
3.5 Risk Evaluation and Prioritization	Ranks risks according to severity, likelihood, impact, and organizational priorities.
3.6 Risk Treatment	Explores risk avoidance, mitigation, transfer, and acceptance.
3.7 Vulnerability Assessment	Identifies, evaluates, and prioritizes weaknesses in systems and configurations.
3.8 Threat Modeling	Analyzes how attackers might compromise a system and identifies appropriate defenses.
3.9 Business Impact Analysis	Evaluates how disruptions affect critical business functions and resources.
3.10 Residual Risk	Explains the risk remaining after security controls are applied.
3.11 Risk Monitoring	Tracks changing threats, vulnerabilities, control effectiveness, and risk levels.
3.12 Third-Party Risk Management	Assesses security risks associated with vendors, contractors, and service providers.

4.5 Security Awareness and Training	Teaches users how to recognize threats and follow secure practices.

Module 5. Risk Management Framework (RMF)
Module description: Introduces the NIST Risk Management Framework and its lifecycle for managing security and privacy risks in information systems.
Lesson / subtopic	Description
5.1 RMF Overview	Explains the purpose, principles, stakeholders, and lifecycle of the NIST RMF.
5.2 Prepare	Establishes organizational context, responsibilities, priorities, and readiness for risk management.
5.3 Categorize	Classifies information and systems according to the impact of a potential compromise.
5.4 Select	Chooses security and privacy controls appropriate to the system's risks and requirements.
5.5 Implement	Deploys selected controls and documents their implementation.
5.6 Assess	Evaluates whether controls are correctly implemented and operating effectively.

5.9 Security Control Documentation	Records control implementations, assessment results, weaknesses, and remediation activities.
5.10 RMF Case Study	Applies the seven RMF steps to a hypothetical information system and documents the decisions.

Module 6. Identity and Access Management
Module description: Teaches how systems identify users, verify their identities, enforce permissions, and record access-related activities.
Lesson / subtopic	Description
6.1 Identification	Explains how users or devices claim an identity, such as through a username or device identifier.
6.2 Authentication	Verifies identity using passwords, security keys, biometrics, or other credentials.
6.3 Authorization	Determines which resources and actions an authenticated entity is permitted to use.
6.4 Accounting and Auditing	Records user activities to support accountability and investigation.
6.5 Access Control Models	Introduces discretionary, mandatory, role-based, and attribute-based access control.

6.7 Multi-Factor Authentication	Requires multiple independent authentication factors to strengthen identity verification.
6.8 Single Sign-On	Allows users to access multiple applications through a shared authentication process.
6.9 Privileged Access Management	Secures administrator accounts and other identities with elevated permissions.
6.10 Principle of Least Privilege	Limits permissions to those necessary for assigned responsibilities.
6.11 Separation of Duties	Divides sensitive responsibilities to reduce errors, fraud, and misuse.
6.12 Zero Trust	Applies explicit verification, least privilege, and ongoing evaluation rather than implicit trust.
6.13 Biometric Authentication	Uses characteristics such as fingerprints or facial features for identity verification.
6.14 Password Management	Covers secure password creation, storage, handling, and authentication practices.


Unit III — Core Technical Security ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Purpose: Develop foundational knowledge of cryptography, networks, endpoints, software, and data protection.

Module 7. Cryptography and Encryption
Module description: Introduces mathematical techniques that protect information and support secure communication, integrity verification, and identity assurance.
Lesson / subtopic	Description
7.1 Cryptographic Fundamentals	Explains plaintext, ciphertext, keys, algorithms, and cryptographic operations.
7.2 Symmetric Encryption	Uses a shared secret key to encrypt and decrypt information.
7.3 Asymmetric Cryptography	Uses public-private key pairs for functions such as key establishment and digital signatures.
7.4 Cryptographic Hash Functions	Produces fixed-length digests for integrity verification and other security purposes.
7.5 Digital Signatures	Provides cryptographic evidence of message integrity and possession of a signing key.
7.6 Digital Certificates	Associates a public key with an identity through a certificate and its trust mechanism.
7.7 Public Key Infrastructure	Manages certificates, public keys, certificate authorities, and trust relationships.
7.8 Key Management	Covers key generation, distribution, storage, rotation, revocation, and destruction.
7.9 Transport Layer Security	Explains how TLS protects communications, including HTTPS connections.
7.10 Encryption at Rest	Protects stored information on disks, databases, backups, and other storage media.
7.11 Encryption in Transit	Protects information as it travels between systems.
7.12 Password Hashing and Salting	Explains how purpose-built password-hashing algorithms and unique salts protect stored passwords.
7.13 Steganography	Covers techniques for concealing information within images, audio, or other media.
7.14 Cryptanalysis	Studies methods used to analyze cryptographic systems and their weaknesses.
7.15 Post-Quantum Cryptography	Introduces cryptographic algorithms designed to resist attacks from quantum computers.

8.8 Wireless Network Security	Protects Wi-Fi networks, access points, wireless authentication, and communications.

8.10 Network Access Control	Enforces requirements for users and devices before granting network access.
8.11 Network Monitoring	Examines network traffic and device activity for suspicious behavior.
8.12 Network Hardening	Applies secure configurations and disables unnecessary services.
8.13 Remote Access Security	Protects remote connections using authentication, access policies, and monitoring.
8.14 Cloud and Software-Defined Network Security	Secures virtual networks, programmable network controls, and cloud connectivity.
